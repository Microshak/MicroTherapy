# Shame

## Common Presentations
- "I am fundamentally flawed / broken / wrong"
- Distinguishing shame ("I am bad") from guilt ("I did something bad")
- Religious or cultural shame around identity, sexuality, or choices
- Perfectionism driven by fear of being "found out"
- Hiding parts of self from others
- Self-criticism that feels automatic and true

## Key Therapeutic Questions
- Where did you learn that this part of you was unacceptable?
- Who taught you that? Did they have their own reasons?
- If someone you loved felt this way, what would you tell them?
- What would it be like to NOT carry this shame for one day?

## Important: Shame-Sensitive Approach
- **Do NOT** directly challenge shame-based beliefs early on — it feels invalidating
- **DO** validate the pain first: "That sounds exhausting to carry."
- **DO** explore where the shame came from before examining whether it's true
- **DO** use externalization: "That shame voice — where did it learn to talk like that?"
- Religious shame: don't challenge faith. Separate tradition from self-worth.

## Modality Guidance
| Modality | Best For |
|----------|----------|
| ACT | Acceptance, defusion from shame thoughts, values |
| CBT | Examining evidence for shame beliefs (gently, later) |
| General Therapy | Validation, creating safety to disclose |
