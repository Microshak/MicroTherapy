# MI — Motivational Interviewing

## When to Use
- Client is ambivalent ("I want to change, but...")
- Motivation is low or inconsistent
- Client resists direct advice
- Behavior change is the goal (habits, substance use, health, relationships)
- Client says "I know I should but I can't"

## When NOT to Use
- Client is ready and asking for a plan (use SFBT)
- Client needs emotional processing (use General Therapy or ACT)
- Immediate crisis (use Crisis Response)

## Core Techniques

### OARS
- **O**pen questions — "What brings you here today?" not "Are you concerned about X?"
- **A**ffirmations — notice strengths, efforts, values
- **R**eflections — simple ("You're feeling stuck") and complex ("It sounds like part of you wants change and part of you is scared of what change means")
- **S**ummaries — collect, link, transition

### Elicit Change Talk
- "What would be different if you made this change?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how important is this change? Why not lower?"
- "What's the best thing that could happen if you changed?"
- "What's the worst thing that could happen if nothing changes?"

### Rolling with Resistance
- Don't argue. Reflect and explore.
- "It sounds like you're not ready to think about changing right now."
- Resistance is a signal to shift approach, not push harder

## Key Principles
- The client already has what they need — your job is to evoke it
- Ambivalence is normal, not pathology
- You don't have to get them to change today. Plant seeds.
