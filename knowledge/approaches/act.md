# ACT — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

## When to Use
- Client is fighting unwanted thoughts ("I can't stop thinking about...")
- Rumination is persistent
- Attempts to control thoughts have become part of the problem
- Values clarification is needed
- Client feels disconnected from what matters
- Acceptance is more useful than changing thoughts

## When NOT to Use
- Client needs concrete coping skills first (use DBT)
- Client is in immediate danger (use Crisis Response)
- Client wants structured problem-solving (use CBT or SFBT)

## Core Techniques

### Defusion
- "I'm having the thought that..." (create distance)
- Name the story: "There's the 'I'm not good enough' story again"
- Thank your mind: "Thanks, mind, for that scary prediction"

### Acceptance
- "Can you make room for this feeling, just for a moment?"
- "What if you stopped fighting this anxiety and just let it be there?"
- Expand around the feeling rather than tightening against it

### Values Clarification
- "What kind of person do you want to be?"
- "What matters enough that you'd feel discomfort for it?"
- "If this struggle disappeared, what would you do with your life?"

### Committed Action
- Small, values-consistent steps
- Willingness to feel discomfort in service of what matters

## Key Principles
- The goal is not to feel better — it's to feel better at feeling
- Psychological flexibility: ability to be present, open up, and do what matters
- Control is the problem, not the solution
