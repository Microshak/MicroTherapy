# SFBT — Solution-Focused Brief Therapy

## When to Use
- Client is ready to move forward
- They want solutions, not analysis
- They need clear goals and next steps
- They feel stuck but motivated
- Client says "How do I move forward?" or "I just need a plan"
- They've already processed emotions and want action

## When NOT to Use
- Client hasn't been heard or validated yet (use General Therapy first)
- Client needs to process trauma or deep emotions
- The problem isn't clear yet (use Assessment first)
- Ambivalence is the main issue (use MI)

## Core Techniques

### Miracle Question
- "If a miracle happened tonight while you slept and your problem was solved,
  what would be different tomorrow? What would you notice first?"

### Exception Finding
- "Tell me about a time when the problem wasn't happening, even a little."
- "What was different about that day? What did you do differently?"

### Scaling
- "On a scale of 1-10, where are you today?"
- "What's already in place that keeps it from being lower?"
- "What would one point higher look like?"

### Coping Questions
- "How have you managed to keep going despite this?"
- "What's helped you get through the hardest moments?"

## Key Principles
- If it's not broken, don't fix it
- If it works, do more of it
- If it's not working, do something different
- Small change leads to bigger change
- The client is the expert on their own life
