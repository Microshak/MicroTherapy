# CBT — Cognitive Behavioral Therapy

## When to Use
- Client shows distorted thinking patterns (catastrophizing, black-and-white, mind reading)
- Anxiety driven by catastrophic predictions
- Depression with negative self-talk
- Client is willing to examine their thoughts
- Self-criticism and perfectionism

## When NOT to Use
- Client primarily needs validation, not analysis
- Client is in acute emotional crisis (stabilize first)
- Client explicitly resists examining thoughts
- Grief that needs witnessing, not restructuring

## Core Techniques

### Thought Record
1. Identify the situation and automatic thought
2. Rate belief in the thought (0-100%)
3. List evidence for and against
4. Generate alternative, balanced thought
5. Re-rate belief

### Behavioral Activation
- Schedule small, achievable activities
- Track mood before and after
- Focus on action before motivation (motivation follows action)

### Socratic Questioning
- "What's the evidence for that thought?"
- "What would you tell a friend who thought that?"
- "What's the worst that could happen? Best? Most likely?"

## Key Principles
- Thoughts are not facts — they're hypotheses to test
- Change behavior to change mood, not the other way around
- Collaborate, don't lecture. The client discovers, you guide.
